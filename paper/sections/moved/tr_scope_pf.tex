\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:scope}]
Term by term. A literal tag with a non-empty value is an equality matcher; a negated one
its negation; a glob is
Proposition~\ref{prop:glob-regex}; a bare key or \texttt{key:*} is presence, which is
\texttt{=\textasciitilde{}".+"} and not \texttt{".*"}, the latter also matching series
carrying no such label; a single-key disjunction is
Proposition~\ref{prop:disjunction}. Conjunction is the comma in both languages.
Injectivity on tag keys is needed because mangling sends \texttt{a.b} and \texttt{a\_b}
to one label name, so a matcher on either selects series carrying the other, and
injectivity on label \emph{sets} does not rule this out --- two colliding keys can sit in
one series, whose label set is then unlike any other and collides with nothing while the
matcher correspondence fails anyway. Injectivity on metric names is needed for the
parallel reason: the selector has to pick out the series $\mathrm{name}(s)=m$ does, and
\texttt{metric\_name} is the same mangling.
Remark~\ref{rem:mangling} measures both classes.

The empty-value hypothesis is the clause this lemma lacked until the eighth review pass,
and it is the one that cannot be discharged by a condition on the target: Datadog
separates three tag states and Prometheus's data model admits two, so where a series
carries a tag with an empty value the presence matchers are wrong by that series and the
bare forms \texttt{k:} and \texttt{!k:} have no counterpart at all.
\S\ref{sec:gaps} reports what the translator does about each --- a refusal for the bare
forms, a caveat for presence --- and \texttt{scripts\slash scope\_empty\_tag.py} measures
all three states against a real account.
\end{proof}
