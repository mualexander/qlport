\paragraph{How much of the shortfall is beyond reach.}
Separating the kinds of refusal is the point of having codes at all, and on this corpus
the separation has an answer this paper did not set out to report: none of it is shown
to be beyond reach. Three kinds are possible and the catalogue keeps them apart --- what
Prometheus does not \emph{hold}, what PromQL cannot \emph{say}, and what this translator
has not \emph{built} --- with a fourth, a query Datadog itself rejects, which
Remark~\ref{rem:third-refusal} describes and which holds nothing demonstrated here
either. The first two columns hold nothing demonstrated. Of the $287$ refused
occurrences, $3.3\%$ of the corpus, all but $14$ are constructs this translator has not
implemented, \texttt{top(...)} chief among them; those $14$ are refused on a hypothesis
this paper tests for the declared metrics and cannot test for theirs
(\S\ref{sec:evaluation}): undecided rather than retracted, and carried here as
everywhere the column is mentioned.

The column did not start empty, and \S\ref{sec:discussion} keeps the running count of
what left it and why, because the pattern is more informative than the total. The last
three to go were the percentile aggregators --- $20$ of their $34$ occurrences, the
other $14$ being the undecided ones above --- the tag-key globs ($31$) and the
scopes separating an absent tag from one present with an empty value ($5$): a corollary
applied where its own hypothesis fails, a construct this paper's normaliser fabricated
out of a template variable, and an information barrier that turns out to decide the
translation rather than to block it.

Because a query is abandoned at its first obstruction (Remark~\ref{rem:one-gap}),
$99.9\%$ is an upper bound on what implementing the unimplemented set would reach rather
than a promise: a refused query could carry a second obstruction behind the first.
Re-reading the $287$ for the other codes' constructs finds none, so the bound is tight
with nothing left over. What separates it from $100\%$ is the $8$ occurrences
\S\ref{sec:grammars}'s grammar does not parse, and an earlier version of this remark
called that ``a fact about this paper's instrument and not about either language''. That
is more than is known. The six distinct queries behind those $8$ are all one construct,
the conditional aggregator \texttt{count(v: v>=0):}, which is in Datadog's own dashboards
and in none of its documentation, and this paper declines to guess its semantics --- so
whether PromQL expresses it is not decided here. The ceiling is better stated as
$99.9\%$ with $8$ occurrences unclassified: the instrument does not reach them, and
nothing in this paper says a translator could. Against the $96.6\%$
measured here, the whole distance to the ceiling is engineering, as the disjunction rule
demonstrated by closing $1.4$ points of it and \texttt{.weighted()} by closing $0.8$
more. That is the finding to carry into \S\ref{sec:discussion}: going this way, every part
of the apparent incompatibility that this paper can classify is work, and what is left
over --- $14$ occurrences undecided and $8$ unparsed --- is small enough to enumerate
query by query.
