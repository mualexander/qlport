\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:reverse}]
Suppose some Datadog query $q$ were such a translation, along some $u$. The witnesses
satisfy $\phi_\rho(D)=\phi_\rho(D')$ --- that is what failing invariance means --- so $q$
receives literally the same database on both, and
$\sem{q}_{\mathrm{DD}}(\phi_\rho(D),u(t))=\sem{q}_{\mathrm{DD}}(\phi_\rho(D'),u(t))$.
Call that common value $y$. Soundness applied at $D$ and at $D'$ gives
$d(\sem{p}_{\mathrm{PQ}}(D,t),y)\le\varepsilon$ and
$d(\sem{p}_{\mathrm{PQ}}(D',t),y)\le\varepsilon$, so by the triangle inequality of
Lemma~\ref{lem:metric},
\[
d\big(\sem{p}_{\mathrm{PQ}}(D,t),\sem{p}_{\mathrm{PQ}}(D',t)\big)\le 2\varepsilon ,
\]
contradicting the hypothesis on $\varepsilon$. No choice of $u$ enters the argument:
whatever instant $q$ is read at, it is read at the same instant on both databases, which
is all the step above uses.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The mirror of Proposition~\ref{prop:admissible}, and deliberately so --- it is the same
triangle inequality, applied with the roles of the two systems exchanged. What changes is
which side the conclusion binds. There the constraint fell on the candidate target
expression, and a translator could satisfy it by choosing a different one. Here it falls
on the \emph{source}, and there is nothing to choose: the query is untranslatable, by
anyone, for as long as Datadog stores $\phi_\rho(D)$ rather than $D$.
