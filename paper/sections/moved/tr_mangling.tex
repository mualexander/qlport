\begin{remark}[The injectivity hypothesis is not vacuous]\label{rem:mangling}
An earlier version of this proof discharged that hypothesis by asserting that no corpus
source contains such a pair, and stated the hypothesis over label sets alone. Both were
wrong, and in opposite directions: the corpus contains three colliding pairs, and on
label sets --- the one class the hypothesis named --- it contains none. The collisions are
in metric names and in a grouping key:
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\texttt{openstack.nova.hypervisor.load\_1} & \texttt{openstack.nova.hypervisor\_load.1}\\
\texttt{rabbitmq.queue.messages.ready} & \texttt{rabbitmq.queue.messages\_ready}\\
\texttt{cluster-name} & \texttt{cluster\_name}\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The five distinct queries using the two metric pairs --- six occurrences --- are all
translated, so two distinct Datadog
metrics are emitted as one PromQL selector. The third pair is a grouping key, and it is
forced rather than incidental: a PromQL label name cannot contain a hyphen, so no
mangling avoids it. That is why Lemma~\ref{lem:scope} states injectivity over the
identifier classes its proof consumes --- metric names and tag keys --- rather than over
label sets: the measured violations all sit outside the class the hypothesis used to
name. This remark said ``now states'' for fourteen passes while the lemma still said
label sets, so the correction was recorded here and not carried there; it is the same
defect as the one Corollary~\ref{cor:rate} carried, and the reason \S\ref{sec:discussion}
treats a correction as unmade until everything resting on it has moved.

The hypothesis is therefore a real condition on the target database and not a formality.
A translation of either \texttt{rabbitmq} query is exact in a database carrying one of
the pair and wrong in a database carrying both, where the selector would sum over series
the source query never saw. The corpus cannot say which, because a corpus of queries is
not a database. What it can say is that the collision rate is low and non-zero: three
pairs among $5{,}389$ metric names and $1{,}264$ tag keys, pinned by test so the claim
cannot drift. The key census is over every key the corpus's scopes and grouping lists
mention, not the grouping lists alone, because the hypothesis is over the keys a matcher
can name: widening it from $723$ to $1{,}264$ added $541$ keys that are only ever
filtered and no further collision.

Reporting it is the same discipline as Remark~\ref{rem:refusal} applied to a hypothesis
rather than to a refusal. The assertion it replaces was checked by reading rather than by
computing, which is how a false empirical claim survived in the proof of the lemma the
soundness theorem rests on.
\end{remark}
