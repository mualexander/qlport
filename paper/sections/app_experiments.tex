\apphead{What the vendor was asked}{app:experiments}

Datadog does not document the behaviour several of this paper's claims rest on, so it was
measured against a trial account and the raw responses cached under \texttt{data/raw/}.
Each experiment below states its design, its prediction table where it has one, and what
came back; the body cites the answers.

Table~\ref{tab:vendor-provenance} says when each measurement was taken and against which
site, generated from the caches rather than written here, because a paper measuring an
unversioned service should date its readings: the behaviour can change without notice,
and a reader who cannot date two figures cannot tell whether they describe one system.
All of them fall in a five-day window, which is the argument that nothing here spans a
change --- an argument from the dates, which is why they are printed rather than
summarised.

One thing the account may itself bear on is not settled here. E4 and E18 read the
automatic rollup interval from a single low-cardinality series, defended by the interval
being a function of the query's time range and not of the data --- which E18 supports,
finding the same interval at a span whether its buckets came back populated or null. But
that is a stronger claim than one series carries, and whether a production account at
high cardinality chooses the same intervals is not something these experiments asked. Two of them are not API experiments:
Remark~\ref{rem:type-measured} asks the vendor's published integration repository
instead, because what the dashboards omit is what that repository declares, and
\S\ref{sec:replay} writes one real request trace into both systems at once. The
section below them indexes what the whole paper rests on, experiment by experiment and
proof by proof.
