\section{Translation (RQ2)}
\label{sec:translation}
RQ2 asks for the largest fragment that translates soundly, which presupposes something
to be sound against. This section supplies it: an intermediate representation with a
denotation, a lowering from the source language into it, an emission from it into the
target, and a statement of what the composite guarantees. The gap catalogue is in
\S\ref{sec:gaps} and the coverage in \S\ref{sec:evaluation}; what belongs here is the
machinery and the one theorem it is for.

The translator is the classical three-stage shape --- parse, lower, emit --- for the
classical reason: it is the only arrangement in which $n$ front ends and $m$ back ends
cost $n+m$ pieces of work instead of $nm$~\citep{aho2006}. That matters here beyond
economy. A direct Datadog-to-PromQL rewriter would have no object to be sound
\emph{about}, so every claim would have to compare two vendor implementations; with an
IR in between, soundness factors into two claims against one definition.

\subsection{A typed series algebra}
\label{sec:ir}

\begin{definition}[IR]\label{def:ir}
The IR is two-sorted. Sort $\mathsf{S}$ is a \emph{set of series}, sort $\mathsf{M}$ a
\emph{set of windowed sample sequences}, and sort $\mathsf{V}$ a \emph{vector}: a finite
set of label-set/value pairs with distinct label sets. The terms are
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{0.40\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Sort & Term & \\
\midrule
$\mathsf{S}$ & $\mathrm{Select}(m,\overline{\mu},\theta)$
  & metric name, label matchers, kind $\theta$ \\
$\mathsf{M}$ & $\mathrm{Window}(e^{\mathsf{S}},w)$ & the samples in $(t-w,t]$ \\
$\mathsf{V}$ & $\mathrm{RangeFn}(f,e^{\mathsf{M}})$ & a function of a window \\
$\mathsf{V}$ & $\mathrm{Rollup}(\mathit{op},\Delta,e^{\mathsf{S}},b)$
  & Datadog's time aggregation; $b$ divides by $\Delta$ \\
$\mathsf{V}$ & $\mathrm{Agg}(\mathit{op},\overline{k},\mathit{mode},e^{\mathsf{V}})$
  & space aggregation, keeping or dropping $\overline{k}$ \\
$\mathsf{V}$ & $\mathrm{BinOp}(\mathit{op},e^{\mathsf{V}},e^{\mathsf{V}},\overline{k})$
  & arithmetic, matched on $\overline{k}$ \\
$\mathsf{V}$ & $\mathrm{Scale}(c,e^{\mathsf{V}})$, $\;\mathrm{Number}(c)$ & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $\theta\in\{\text{counter},\text{gauge},\text{unknown}\}$,
$\mathit{op}\in\{\mathsf{sum},\mathsf{avg},\mathsf{min},\mathsf{max},\mathsf{count}\}$,
and a matcher $\mu$ is one of $k=v$, $k\neq v$, $k=\!\sim\! r$, $k\neq\!\sim\! r$.

Three things about $\theta$. It is written $\theta$ and not $\kappa$, which is
Definition~\ref{def:kappa}'s coverage and appeared in this section meaning both. It is an
\emph{annotation} the lowering infers and the emitter reads, not a constraint on which
series a $\mathrm{Select}$ denotes --- the denotation ignores it, so two
$\mathrm{Select}$s differing only in $\theta$ are denotationally equal, and what makes the
kind load-bearing is hypothesis~(3) of Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} rather than the
semantics. And $\text{unknown}$ is the common case, because no method in the query text
settles the type; Remark~\ref{rem:gauge} is about what the emitter does with it.
A term is \emph{well sorted} if each argument has the sort its position requires; only
$\mathsf{V}$ terms are complete queries.
\end{definition}

Each sort has the denotation it looks like it should (\apref{app:proofs}): a
$\mathrm{Select}$ is the set of series whose name and labels match, a $\mathrm{Rollup}$
reduces each of those series over $[t,t+\Delta)$, an $\mathrm{Agg}$ groups the resulting
vector by $\overline{k}$ and reduces each group, and $\mathrm{Number}$, $\mathrm{Scale}$
and $\mathrm{BinOp}$ are pointwise on matching label sets. One choice in it is not
presentational: \texttt{.as\_rate()}'s divisor sits on the space aggregation rather than
on the rollup, because Datadog divides the aggregated result and the two placements
differ for $\mathit{op}=\mathsf{count}$ --- measured rather than chosen
(\apref{app:experiments}).

\inlineonly{\noindent}%
Two features of Definition~\ref{def:ir} are load-bearing rather than tidy. First,
$\mathrm{Rollup}$ is the composite of $\mathrm{Window}$ and a reduction, which is why it
goes from $\mathsf{S}$ to $\mathsf{V}$ in one step: Datadog has no term for a window on
its own, so an IR that separated them would offer the front end a choice the source
language does not have. $\mathrm{Window}$ and $\mathrm{RangeFn}$ remain because PromQL
does separate them, and the Datadog-to-PromQL emitter accordingly refuses both --- they
are reserved for the reverse direction. No corpus query reaches them, which is the
status the fourth kind of refusal has in \S\ref{sec:gaps} --- that catalogue's own rows
are all reached, and Remark~\ref{rem:third-refusal} is where an empty one is discussed.

Second, the sorts make \S\ref{sec:ingestion}'s Corollary~\ref{cor:raw} structural.
A complete query has sort $\mathsf{V}$, and the only terms of sort $\mathsf{S}$ are
$\mathrm{Select}$s, which can appear solely as the argument of a $\mathrm{Window}$ or a
$\mathrm{Rollup}$. So a well-sorted IR term \emph{cannot} present a bare selector to the
emitter: the corollary said no sound translation reads absolute counter values, and the
type discipline is what enforces it, with no check to forget to run.

\subsection{Where the rollup sits}
\label{sec:rollup-order}
One structural fact about Datadog decides the shape of every translated query, and it is
easy to get backwards. A Datadog query is evaluated first by time and then by
space~\citep{ddnested}: the rollup reduces each series on its own, and the space
aggregator (\texttt{sum:}, \texttt{avg:}, \texttt{max:}, \texttt{min:},
\texttt{count:}, with its optional \texttt{by \{...\}}) then combines those reduced
values. PromQL's range functions have the same shape, so the translation is direct:
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\texttt{max:x\{*\}.rollup(avg, 60)}\quad$\longmapsto$\quad\texttt{max(avg\_over\_time(x[60s]))}
\end{center}

Nesting the two layers the other way round --- space aggregation first, rollup outside
--- describes a different query, and raises a question the real semantics never asks:
whether the two aggregators commute. They do for $(\mathsf{sum},\mathsf{sum})$,
$(\mathsf{max},\mathsf{max})$ and $(\mathsf{min},\mathsf{min})$, and not for
$\mathsf{max}$ or $\mathsf{count}$ over an averaging rollup, where PromQL would need a
subquery to put the rollup outside. An earlier version of this translator was built that
way and refused $348$ query occurrences --- four points of $\kappa$ --- on the strength
of that commutation argument. Every translation it \emph{did} emit was still correct,
because where the aggregators commute the two orders coincide, which is exactly what
makes the error hard to notice: the failure shows up only in the refusals.

\subsection{Lowering}
\label{sec:lowering}

Lowering is a syntax-directed translation in the sense of \citet{lewis1968}: one bottom-up
pass over the Datadog parse tree, each node's IR computed from its children's. Two
attributes break that pattern, and both break it because the information is not in the
query.

The first is the metric's \textbf{kind}. Whether a Datadog metric was submitted as COUNT
or GAUGE decides which rule its rollup may use, and a dashboard's JSON never records it.
What the query carries is evidence rather than a statement: \texttt{.as\_count()},
\texttt{.as\_rate()} and a \texttt{.rollup(sum, $\Delta$)} are all documented for COUNT
metrics, so the front end reads any of them as the type the query does not give. The
evidence is weaker than it looks and Remark~\ref{rem:gauge} measures how much weaker;
what matters here is the shape of the inference, not its strength. The
method chain is a \emph{sibling} of the scope, and the kind belongs to the
$\mathrm{Select}$ beneath it, so the attribute is synthesised at the query node and
inherited downward --- an attribute grammar in Knuth's sense~\citep{knuth1968} rather
than a bottom-up transduction. Absent any such evidence the kind stays $\text{unknown}$
and the back end is left to say what it rests on.

The second is the rollup \textbf{interval}. Datadog derives it from the dashboard's time
range when \texttt{.rollup} does not give one, and the query text does not contain the
time range. The IR therefore stores $\Delta=\bot$ and the back end resolves it, so
$\mathrm{Rollup}$ is the one node whose denotation is undefined until a parameter outside
the IR is supplied. This is not elegant and the alternative is worse: inventing a default
during lowering would bury an assumption in the IR, where nothing downstream could see
that it had been made.

Lowering also renames. Prometheus admits a narrower identifier alphabet than Datadog
does, so a metric name or tag key is \emph{mangled} on the way across: the separators
Datadog allows and Prometheus does not --- a dot, a hyphen, a space --- become
underscores, and the result is the label or metric name the emitter writes. The mangling
is the one step of lowering that can lose information, since two source identifiers can
arrive at one target identifier, which is why both soundness theorems below hypothesise
that it is injective on the identifiers they range over and why
Remark~\ref{rem:mangling} measures whether it is.

Lowering abandons a query at its first untranslatable construct, so the catalogue of
\S\ref{sec:gaps} counts \emph{first} refusals and its per-code counts are lower bounds
on how many queries each construct affects. The total is unaffected --- every refused
query is counted exactly once --- so neither $\kappa$ nor the ordering of
\S\ref{sec:staircase} depends on which refusal came first (\apref{app:proofs}).

\subsection{Counter rollups}
The rules for \texttt{.as\_count()}, \texttt{.as\_rate()} and \texttt{.rollup(sum,
$\Delta$)} are established in Section~\ref{sec:ingestion}, where they belong: which
PromQL expressions may translate a Datadog rollup at all is a question about the
ingestion mapping, not about the emitter. Propositions~\ref{prop:offset}
and~\ref{prop:anchored} give the two exact forms and Table~\ref{tab:rollup-live} the
measurements. What the emitter adds is the choice between them and the preconditions it
reports: \texttt{COUNTER\_RESET} on the cumulative difference,
\texttt{ANCHORED\_VERSION} on the anchored form, and \texttt{ROLLUP\_ALIGNMENT} on
both.

\subsection{Disjunctive scopes}
\label{sec:disjunction}

A Datadog scope is a boolean query over tags; a PromQL selector is a \emph{conjunction}
of matchers. Conjunction, negation and glob matching therefore cross over term by term,
and disjunction has no selector-level form --- in general. It has one in the case that
matters:

\begin{proposition}[Single-key disjunction]\label{prop:disjunction}
Let $k$ be a tag key, let $g_1,\dots,g_n$ be Datadog globs or literals, and let $r_i$ be
the regular expression $\mathrm{glob}(g_i)$ of \S\ref{sec:regular}. Then the scope
$\{k{:}g_1 \vee \cdots \vee k{:}g_n\}$ --- equivalently the membership form
$\{k \in (g_1,\dots,g_n)\}$ --- denotes the same series set as a single PromQL matcher on
$k$ whose regular expression is the alternation $r_1|\cdots|r_n$; the negated scope is
the same alternation under \texttt{!\textasciitilde{}} instead of
\texttt{=\textasciitilde{}}.
\end{proposition}

What the proposition rests on is where the anchors go --- Prometheus anchors an
alternation over the whole value, which is read off a real server rather than asserted
(\texttt{scripts\slash verify\_matchers.py}) --- and how much of the corpus the
single-key case reaches, which turns on Datadog's \texttt{or} binding more loosely than
its comma --- put to the account rather than read off the manual
(Remark~\ref{rem:precedence}) --- and on a presence branch subsuming every value
(\apref{app:proofs}).

\iffull\input{sections/moved/tr_disjunction_detail}\fi

What remains refused is every disjunction whose branches do not all constrain
\emph{one} key --- across different keys, or within a branch that constrains two at
once --- which \S\ref{sec:gaps} measures at four query occurrences in the corpus, sorted into
their three shapes \apin{app:eval}. Those stay sayable in
PromQL --- as a set union of selectors with the rollup distributed over the branches,
$2^k$ selectors for $k$ disjunctions --- but not as the syntax-directed translation of a
scope, which is what this translator is.

\subsection{Emission, and conditional exactness}
\label{sec:emission}

Emission is structural recursion on the IR with no choices left to make, save one: the
reset-aware \texttt{anchored} form of \S\ref{sec:ingestion} or the plain cumulative
difference. The output is not a string but a pair.

\begin{definition}[Emission]\label{def:emission}
$E(e)$ is either $\bot$ with one gap, or a pair $(p,\,C)$ of a PromQL expression and a
set $C$ of \emph{caveats}: conditions under which $p$ is exact, which the emitter
observed itself needing and which the IR does not record as satisfied.
\end{definition}

\noindent
The caveats are not a disclaimer. They are the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} below, collected by the only component in a position to know
which ones a given query incurred, and \S\ref{sec:caveats} reports how often each is
incurred. Seven codes arise, over six conditions --- the fourth has a code for each of
the two forms it chooses between, which is why Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} has six
hypotheses and the emitter seven codes:
\texttt{ROLLUP\_ALIGNMENT} on every rollup, since Datadog's buckets are epoch-aligned,
left-closed and named by their start (Remark~\ref{rem:closure}) while a PromQL window is
right-closed and follows the evaluation step;
\texttt{ROLLUP\_DEFAULT} when the interval was assumed;
\texttt{COUNTER\_RESET} on the cumulative difference, or \texttt{ANCHORED\_VERSION} on the
form that avoids it; \texttt{PHI\_SERIES\_START} on the forms that read a counter, which
need a sample older than the bucket and which the anchored form again avoids
(Remark~\ref{rem:series-start}); \texttt{TAG\_PRESENCE\_EMPTY} where the selector asks a
tag merely to be present, because Datadog separates an absent tag from one present with
an empty value and Prometheus's data model does not; and
\texttt{METRIC\_TYPE\_UNKNOWN}, which deserves its own paragraph.

Two measurements settle what \texttt{METRIC\_TYPE\_UNKNOWN} costs, and both are
uncomfortable. The first is that the condition cannot be discharged from the query text.
Datadog accepts \texttt{.as\_count()} and \texttt{.as\_rate()} on a \texttt{GAUGE} and
silently ignores them, so a modifier records what a dashboard's author took the metric to
be rather than what it is; it is evidence about a person, not about a type. With that
premise withdrawn the caveat holds of all $8{,}475$ translated occurrences --- $100\%$ of
them (Table~\ref{tab:translation-caveats}). The second is that it can nonetheless be
checked, because the repository the corpus came from declares what the dashboards do not:
beside each integration's \texttt{assets\slash dashboards\slash},
\texttt{integrations-core} ships a \texttt{metadata.csv} with a \texttt{metric\_type}
column. Joining the two, $7{,}588$ of the translated occurrences --- $89.5\%$ --- name a
metric the repository declares as a gauge or a count, and the front end's inference
disagrees with the declaration on $491$ of them: $6.5\%$ of the decidable set, $5.6\%$ of
the corpus. The two directions of error are both present and are different failures. In
$242$ occurrences the metric is a \texttt{count} and the query carries no evidence of it,
so the emitted \texttt{*\_over\_time} reduces running totals where Datadog reduces deltas
--- unrelated rather than approximate. In $249$ the metric is a \texttt{gauge} and the
query carries a counter modifier anyway, so the emitter chooses a counter rule for a
series with no deltas to difference. Recording this does not move $\kappa$ --- the
translations are unchanged --- which is the point: it changes what they are claimed to
be. Both designs, what came back, and what a disagreement is and is not evidence of are
set out \apin{app:experiments}.

\subsection{What the composite guarantees}
\label{sec:kernel-soundness}

The fragment that carries the corpus is the one \S\ref{sec:staircase} orders first:
a space aggregation of a rollup of a selector. Call it the \emph{kernel},
\[
\mathcal{K}\;::=\;\mathrm{Agg}(\mathit{op}_s,\overline{k},\mathsf{by},\
\mathrm{Rollup}(\mathit{op}_t,\Delta,\mathrm{Select}(m,\overline{\mu},\theta),\mathit{per\_second})).
\]
The $\mathit{per\_second}$ flag is written out because Definition~\ref{def:ir} carries it
and because $258$ of the kernel's occurrences set it: an earlier version of this display
listed three of $\mathrm{Rollup}$'s four arguments, which put every \texttt{.as\_rate()}
rollup outside the kernel as literally defined while the counts included them; the
version that fixed the count then wrote the flag and the series in the wrong order, so
no term of $\mathcal{K}$ was well sorted either, which is the same defect caught twice. The
proof \apin{app:proofs} treats the case.

\begin{lemma}[Scopes and selectors agree]\label{lem:scope}
For every Datadog scope $\sigma$ that lowering does not refuse, the matchers
$\overline{\mu}$ it produces select the same series as $\sigma$, provided the
name-mangling of \S\ref{sec:lowering} is injective on $D$'s metric \emph{names} and on
the tag \emph{keys} occurring in $D$ and in $\overline{\mu}$, and provided no series in
$D$ carries an empty value for a tag that $\overline{\mu}$ tests merely for presence.
(The proviso was universal over tags until the tenth pass, which made it strictly
stronger than the hypothesis Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} supplies when it invokes this
lemma. The injectivity class is tag keys and not label \emph{sets}, which is what this
lemma said for fourteen passes after Remark~\ref{rem:mangling} had recorded that label
sets are the wrong class and that the one measured key collision sits outside them:
injectivity on label sets does not stop two colliding keys \emph{within} one series from
being merged, which is exactly the failure the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:scope} turns on.)
\end{lemma}

\iffull\input{sections/moved/tr_mangling}\fi

\begin{theorem}[Kernel soundness]\label{thm:kernel}
Let $q\in\Lang(G_{\mathrm{DD}})$ be a Datadog query whose lowering lands in the
kernel, $L(q)\in\mathcal{K}$, let $E(L(q))=(p,C)$, and let $t$ be an evaluation time. If
\begin{enumerate}
\item $t$ is a multiple of $\Delta$, and the emitted expression is evaluated at
      $u(t)=t+\Delta-\eta$ for an $\eta$ inside Proposition~\ref{prop:offset}'s bound,
      which is not the scrape interval and was once written as though it were
      (Remark~\ref{rem:eta})
      --- not at $t$ and not at $t+\Delta$
      \hfill (\texttt{ROLLUP\_ALIGNMENT})
\item the $\Delta$ the emitter wrote into the range selector is the width of the bucket
      Datadog reports, which holds when the query states the interval and is what
      \texttt{ROLLUP\_DEFAULT} says is \emph{assumed} when it does not
      \hfill (\texttt{ROLLUP\_DEFAULT})
\item the metric's type is the one the query's methods imply: a \texttt{COUNT} where the
      query carries \texttt{.as\_count()}, \texttt{.as\_rate()} or
      \texttt{.rollup(sum,$\Delta$)}, a \texttt{GAUGE} otherwise
      \hfill (\texttt{METRIC\_TYPE\_UNKNOWN})
\item where the emitted form is the cumulative difference: no series resets in
      $[t,t+\Delta)$, or the anchored form was emitted \emph{and} the target server runs
      with extended range selectors enabled --- a condition on the deployment rather
      than on the data, and without it the expression does not evaluate at all
      \hfill (\texttt{COUNTER\_RESET}, \texttt{ANCHORED\_VERSION})
\item where the emitted form reads a counter: every series has a sample before $t$
      \hfill (\texttt{PHI\_SERIES\_START})
\item where the emitted selector asks a tag merely to be present: no series in $D$
      carries that tag with an empty value, which is Lemma~\ref{lem:scope}'s last
      proviso
      \hfill (\texttt{TAG\_PRESENCE\_EMPTY})
\end{enumerate}
and the name mangling of \S\ref{sec:lowering} is injective on $D$'s metric names and on
the tag keys occurring in $D$, in the emitted matchers and in $q$'s grouping list, and
Prometheus's lookback covers the distances the
form emitted for $\theta$ requires --- the two of
Proposition~\ref{prop:offset} under hypothesis~(4)'s first branch, and under its second
the single wider one of Proposition~\ref{prop:anchored}, which is of order $\Delta$ and
is the condition a real server was measured failing --- then
$\sem{q}_{\mathrm{DD}}(\phi_{\mathsf{zero}}(D),t)=\sem{p}_{\mathrm{PQ}}(D,u(t))$ ---
exactly, not within the $\varepsilon$ of Definition~\ref{def:sound}. In the language of
that definition the kernel is exact along $u(t)=t+\Delta-\eta$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
The six numbered hypotheses carry exactly the seven codes
of $C$; the injectivity and the lookback are not, and that asymmetry is worth stating rather than hiding in
the list. Those two are conditions on the \emph{target deployment} ---
on which identifiers collide once mangled, and on how the server is configured --- and
the emitter cannot observe either, so no code records them. Being a condition on the
deployment does not by itself make a condition unobservable, which is why the two ideas
are worth separating: hypothesis~(4)'s anchored branch is also a fact about how the
server was started, and \texttt{ANCHORED\_VERSION} does record it, because it is the
emitter that chose the form that needs it. $C$ is therefore what the
translator can tell you about a translation, not everything the translation needs.
Remark~\ref{rem:mangling} shows the injectivity is not vacuous on this corpus: three
pairs of identifiers collide once mangled, among $5{,}389$ metric names and
$1{,}264$ tag keys --- every key the corpus's scopes and grouping lists mention,
which is the class the hypothesis is over. The
lookback is not a misconfiguration clause, which an earlier version of this theorem
called it: it fails on a \emph{gap in the data} --- a scrape outage, a replaced target ---
at any server configuration, and Proposition~\ref{prop:anchored}'s proof reads an
instance off a real server. Both are unreported either way.

\inlineonly{\noindent}%
The theorem is a statement about the \emph{kernel}, which is $95.7\%$ of the corpus and
$99.0\%$ of what the translator emits, but not all of it --- two figures rather than
one, and Remark~\ref{rem:kernel-figures} is about the third an earlier draft quoted. What the remainder does is
arithmetic between kernel queries, and \S\ref{sec:binop-soundness} extends the theorem
over exactly that. The honest
summary is that the fragment the corpus overwhelmingly uses is exact under six stated
conditions, four of which are properties of the evaluation grid, the deployment and the
data rather than of either language, while two --- the rollup interval and the metric's
type --- are facts a dashboard does not record. That pair is what
\S\ref{sec:discussion} builds its argument on, and the extension below adds no seventh
\emph{code}, though it does carry a hypothesis of its own.

That is also the answer to the question RQ2 was posed to avoid: the preconditions do not
make the translation approximate. Conditions (3), (4), (5) and (6) are the ones that can fail
silently on real data --- the first because nothing in the query settles the type, the
other three because they are statements about data the translator never sees --- and
\S\ref{sec:caveats} measures how often each is in play.

\subsection{Arithmetic between queries}
\label{sec:binop-soundness}

Outside $\mathcal{K}$ the corpus does one thing: arithmetic. A widget divides one
aggregated query by another, scales one by a constant, or subtracts two. Extending
Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} over that is not a matter of applying it pointwise, because the
two languages decide \emph{which} pairs of series an operator applies to by different
rules, and neither rule is documented. Both are therefore measured
(\texttt{scripts\slash binop\_matching.py}; \apref{app:proofs}).

\begin{definition}[Arithmetic closure]\label{def:arith}
$\mathcal{A}$ is the smallest set of IR terms containing $\mathcal{K}$ and every
$\mathrm{Number}(c)$, closed under $\mathrm{Scale}(c,\cdot)$ and
$\mathrm{BinOp}(\oplus,\cdot,\cdot,\overline{k})$ for $\oplus\in\{+,-,\times,\div\}$,
written with all four of Definition~\ref{def:ir}'s arguments: the matching key list is
the fourth, and dropping it here --- as this definition and
Definition~\ref{def:keyset} did --- is the same arity slip the $\mathrm{Rollup}$ display
above reports having made twice.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Key set]\label{def:keyset}
$\chi(e)$ is $\star$ for $\mathrm{Number}$; $\overline{k}$ for
$\mathrm{Agg}(\mathit{op},\overline{k},\mathsf{by},\cdot)$; $\chi(e')$ for
$\mathrm{Scale}(c,e')$; and for $\mathrm{BinOp}(\oplus,l,r,\cdot)$ it is $\chi(r)$ when
$\chi(l)=\star$, $\chi(l)$ when $\chi(r)=\star$, and otherwise the larger of $\chi(l)$
and $\chi(r)$ when one contains the other, and undefined when neither does.
It is undefined on an $\mathrm{Agg}$ in $\mathsf{without}$ mode, which
Definition~\ref{def:ir} admits and $\mathcal{A}$ does not contain: the emitter never
reaches one, since lowering refuses the Datadog construct that would produce it, so the
omission costs nothing and is stated rather than left to be discovered.
\end{definition}

\noindent
$\chi$ is what the emitter computes, so the case analysis below is the one the code
performs rather than a description of it.

\begin{lemma}[How Datadog pairs series]\label{lem:dd-matching}
Let $q_1,q_2\in\mathcal{A}$ have results $r_1,r_2$ at $t$ with
$\chi(q_i)=\overline{k_i}$, and suppose $\overline{k_2}\subseteq\overline{k_1}$. Then
$q_1\oplus q_2$ has domain
$\{\lambda\in\mathrm{dom}(r_1) : \pi_{\overline{k_2}}(\lambda)\in\mathrm{dom}(r_2)\}$
and value $r_1(\lambda)\oplus r_2(\pi_{\overline{k_2}}(\lambda))$. The mirror holds with
the roles exchanged when $\overline{k_1}\subseteq\overline{k_2}$, which has to be stated
rather than left to symmetry because $-$ and $\div$ do not commute, and it is the
orientation \apref{app:proofs}'s use of \texttt{group\_right()} appeals to. If neither
key set contains the other, Datadog rejects the query.
\end{lemma}

\iffull\input{sections/moved/tr_dd_matching_pf}\fi

\begin{lemma}[How PromQL pairs series]\label{lem:pq-matching}
For instant vectors $u,v$: $u\oplus v$ under default matching pairs elements with
\emph{identical} label sets and drops the rest. $u \oplus \texttt{on}(\overline{k})\ 
\texttt{group\_left()}\ v$ pairs each element of $u$ with the element of $v$ agreeing
with it on $\overline{k}$, keeps $u$'s labels, and requires at most one such $v$;
\texttt{group\_right()} is the mirror image. An aggregation drops every label outside its
\texttt{by} list, \emph{including} \texttt{\_\_name\_\_}.
\end{lemma}

\iffull\input{sections/moved/tr_pq_matching_pf}\fi

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic soundness]\label{thm:arith}
Let $q\in\Lang(G_{\mathrm{DD}})$ with $L(q)\in\mathcal{A}$ and
$E(L(q))=(p,C)\neq\bot$, suppose every kernel subterm of $L(q)$
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} at $t$, and suppose they all
carry the same $\Delta$ and admit a common $\eta$. Then
$\sem{q}_{\mathrm{DD}}(\phi_{\mathsf{zero}}(D),t)=\sem{p}_{\mathrm{PQ}}(D,u(t))$ for the
common $u(t)=t+\Delta-\eta$, and $C$ is
the union of those subterms' caveats --- the extension introduces no caveat \emph{code}
of its own, though the shared $\Delta$ is a condition of its own and is not in $C$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
The shared $\Delta$ is a hypothesis rather than an observation, and it was missing. A
PromQL expression is evaluated at one instant while Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel} fixes one
per subterm, so two kernel subterms with different intervals cannot both satisfy it. The
common $\eta$ beside it was missing for the same reason and longer: hypothesis~(1) bounds
$\eta$ by $\min(b+\Delta-t_m,\ b-t_k)$, whose two distances are properties of the
subterm's own series, so equal $\Delta$s do not give equal $\eta$s. It costs nothing to
supply --- the bounds are upper bounds and there are finitely many subterms, so
$\eta=\min_i\eta_i$ satisfies every one of them --- but it has to be supplied, and the
paragraph that congratulated itself on catching this for $\Delta$ had left it open for
$\eta$. For
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\texttt{sum:a\{*\}.as\_count().rollup(sum, 60)\ /\ sum:b\{*\}.as\_count().rollup(sum, 300)}
\end{center}
no instant returns Datadog's reading. The theorem is vacuous there rather than false, and
the corpus contains no such query --- $0$ of the $79$ occurrences carrying a binary
operator mix intervals --- so the measured claim is unaffected and only the sentence
below about every front-end term needed narrowing.

\inlineonly{\noindent}%
Theorem~\ref{thm:arith} reaches further than it may look. Every term the \emph{front
end} can build lies in $\mathcal{A}$, and that is where the universal comes from ---
not from the emitter, which would happily aggregate anything handed to it, and not from
this corpus. Lowering constructs an $\mathrm{Agg}$ at exactly one place, always over a
$\mathrm{Rollup}$ of a $\mathrm{Select}$, which is $\mathcal{K}$; its only other
constructors are $\mathrm{Number}$, $\mathrm{Scale}$ and $\mathrm{BinOp}$ over terms it
has already lowered, which is the closure. $\mathrm{Window}$ and $\mathrm{RangeFn}$ are
reachable from neither, and the emitter refuses them in any case. The corpus then checks
what the construction establishes: $\mathcal{A}$ covers $8{,}475$ occurrences, $96.6\%$
of the corpus and $100\%$ of what was emitted. So the gap between what is claimed and
what is shipped is empty rather than small. That is the statement \S\ref{sec:framework}'s
Remark~\ref{rem:two-kappas} wanted and could not previously make: $\kappa$ counts
translations the emitter is willing to produce, and each of them is now covered by a
proof as well as by the corpus-scale test of \S\ref{sec:differential}.
