\title{\textbf{A Dashboard Query Does Not Say What It Means:}\\
\Large Switching Costs in Observability Query Translation}
\author{Marshall Uy Alexander\\ \small CS 681 Applications of Computability --- Research Paper}
\date{\small \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}\noindent
Teams stay with a proprietary observability vendor less because of their data than
because of their dashboards: thousands of saved queries that a migration must rewrite by
hand, with no way to tell whether a rewrite preserved what the original meant. We asked
whether that cost is a syntactic accident or a semantic necessity, and built the
apparatus to find out --- a grammar for each language, an intermediate representation with
a denotation, soundness stated relative to an ingestion mapping because the two systems do
not store the same thing, and a translator whose every refusal carries a named code.

Run over the $8{,}770$ Datadog metric queries of a corpus of $22{,}894$ extracted from
published dashboard and alerting documents, that translator reaches $96.6\%$ of
occurrences, and the column we had reserved for constructs beyond PromQL's reach emptied
of everything we can demonstrate, bar $14$ occurrences left undecided. On that evidence
the answer to the question as posed is negative: leaving is engineering.

The finding this paper is named for is the one that apparatus was not built to see. A
dashboard query does not determine its own meaning. $97.0\%$ of the translations we emit
are of queries that leave the rollup interval to the dashboard's time range, and where a
widget pins that range the obvious default is wrong for $332$ of $496$ occurrences --- two
times in three, and three times too wide at the modal span. All of them assume a metric
type no query in either language states, and against the vendor's own declarations that
inference is wrong for $6.5\%$ of the cases it can settle: $491$ panels a migration would
carry across believing them exact. Nor is this one vendor's doing --- $88.6\%$ of Datadog
and $82.5\%$ of PromQL query occurrences carry a template variable, and $55.6\%$ of PromQL
range selectors take their window from a dashboard variable rather than a duration. An
independent vendor's translator, run over the same corpus, supplies a $[5m]$ window to
$21.3\%$ of occurrences under the note ``adjust interval as needed'': the same finding,
reached by somebody not looking for it.

Every instrument here is blind to that. A query that does not fix its own meaning parses,
translates, and is counted as covered, so neither the grammars nor the gap catalogue nor
$\kappa$ can see it. The object a migration ports is a query schema plus a resolution
layer belonging to the dashboard tool, which is where the switching cost we measured
actually sits --- and where a common query language would not reach.
\end{abstract}
