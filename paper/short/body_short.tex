\title{\textbf{Leaving Costs Work, Returning Costs Expressiveness:}\\
\Large An Asymmetry in Observability Query Translation}
\author{Marshall Uy Alexander\\ \small CS 681 Applications of Computability --- Research Paper}
\date{\small \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}\noindent
Teams stay with a proprietary observability vendor less because of their data than
because of their dashboards: thousands of saved queries that a migration must rewrite by
hand, with no way to tell whether a rewrite preserved what the original meant. We asked
whether that cost is a syntactic accident or a semantic necessity. The answer depends on
the direction, and the asymmetry is the result.

Forward --- Datadog metric queries into PromQL --- it is an accident. Over the $8{,}770$
Datadog metric queries of a corpus of $22{,}894$ extracted from published dashboard and
alerting documents, $96.6\%$ of occurrences translate, and the column we had reserved for
constructs beyond the target language's reach emptied of everything we can demonstrate,
bar $14$ occurrences left undecided. What remains is work this translator has not done
rather than expressiveness PromQL lacks.

Backward it is a necessity, and provably so. A Datadog tag filter is a glob and a PromQL
tag filter is a regular expression, and globs are a proper subclass. We prove that a glob
language is either a singleton or unbounded in length; that no glob denotes a finite
language with two or more words, nor $\Sigma^{+}$, nor $A^{+}$ for a proper non-empty
$A\subsetneq\Sigma$; and that an infinite language omitting some character of the
alphabet is not a union of finitely many glob languages, which is the one bound-free
result here. We then show that glob-expressibility of a regular expression is decidable,
by an argument about shortest words, with a witness when one exists --- and run that
procedure over every concrete pattern in the corpus, where it agrees with the
syntax-directed translator on all $184$ distinct patterns. Of the $1{,}041$ concrete
matcher occurrences a Datadog scope reaches $91.9\%$, but two thirds of that only as a
union of globs, and of the $84$ refusals $74$ rest on a theorem about single globs
together with a branch bound we choose and state, $10$ on a theorem about globs outright.

Leaving costs work; returning costs expressiveness. One finding of the complete paper is
deliberately not carried here, and \S\ref{s:sec:asymmetry} says what it is: a dashboard
query does not determine its own meaning, and no coverage number can see it.
\end{abstract}
